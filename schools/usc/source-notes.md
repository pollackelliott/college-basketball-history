# USC source notes and provenance

## Primary institutional source

- **USC 2025-26 Men's Basketball Media Guide / Record Book** (`FINAL_25-26_USC_Media_Guide(1).pdf`), owner supplied. The year-by-year results pages are the controlling historical game ledger through 2024-25; the all-time opponent-series section is used as reciprocal USC-published evidence for exact dates and H/A/N when uniquely matchable. The guide also supplies program-history, conference, postseason, and 2007-08 vacatur context.
- **USC Athletics 2025-26 official schedule/results**, used to append the completed 18-14 competitive season and to exclude the two exhibitions.

## Conference and postseason sources

- Owner-supplied **Conference Tournament Site Reference (20260901-204513)**: used only within the owner-authorized USC-relevant Pac-10/Pac-12/Big Ten physical-site boundary. Workbook instructions explicitly distinguish centralized sites from campus preliminary rounds and warn that site rows do not establish H/A/N.
- Pac-12 institutional conference history: USC joined the Pacific Coast Conference in 1922; USC was an original AAWU member in 1959; the conference adopted Pacific-8 in 1968 and Pacific-10 in 1978. USC's own 1921-22 basketball summary already carries a conference record, so the basketball chronology begins PCC in 1921-22.
- USC Athletics 2026 Big Ten Tournament game notes: cross-checks USC's historical NCAA/NIT/NCIT result universe and 2021 Indianapolis NCAA building assignments.
- USC Athletics 2025 College Basketball Crown material and box scores: both USC Crown games were played at MGM Grand Garden Arena in Las Vegas/Paradise.

## HOME venue evidence reviewed

USC Athletics' 2005-06/2006-07 historical facility notes state that the Sports Arena had been USC's main home since 1959 and identify alternate home sites, while the 2006-07 guide lists 13 pre-Galen home locations known from records dating to 1927-28. USC Athletics establishes Galen Center as the home beginning in 2006-07. Archival facility/history research establishes Pan-Pacific Auditorium as USC's 1949-59 home and Shrine Auditorium as a major 1940s home site. Exact pre-1949 game-to-building assignments remain unresolved where surviving evidence does not distinguish among USC's documented buildings.

## NCAA physical-site research

Every NCAA row was checked against USC institutional postseason notes, institutional game releases, tournament host histories, and/or established project physical venue identities. Important disambiguations include Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum vs. Philadelphia's First Union Center in 2001; ARCO Arena in Sacramento in 2002; Charlotte Coliseum in 1997; and the 2021 Indianapolis sequence of Bankers Life Fieldhouse, Hinkle Fieldhouse, Bankers Life Fieldhouse, and Lucas Oil Stadium. NCAA city text in the all-time opponent tables is not blindly trusted where USC's dedicated/current postseason evidence is more specific.

## Exact-date recovery discipline

Blank year-by-year dates were filled only when USC's own all-time opponent-series entry supplied a full exact date and the season/opponent/score/result match was unique. Year-only and season-only series entries were not promoted to exact dates. Known source-internal bad year/date renderings in modern series tables are not used to overwrite the chronological ledger.

## Bounded site-debt validation sources

The post-freeze site-debt audit additionally used Stanford Athletics' official `Year-By-Year Results` reciprocal history to resolve USC/Stanford H/A/N row by row from Stanford's `USC` / `at USC` wording and explicit neutral-site notes. Modern Stanford schedule archive pages corroborate the same site direction. Nebraska Athletics' official series table resolves the 2025-01-22 USC game as Nebraska home. USC's own all-time opponent-series table resolves Washington (1964), Princeton (1972), and California (1995, 1996) site classifications; USC's raw 1975 Hawai'i line itself says `at Hawai'i`. Contemporary Los Angeles Times reporting identifies Cal State Dominguez Hills as USC's temporary home court for the January 1985 Washington/Washington State games, and Cal State Dominguez Hills Athletics identifies its basketball gymnasium as the Torodome.
