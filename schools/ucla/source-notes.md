# UCLA source notes

## Principal source

**2025-26 UCLA Men's Basketball Media Guide / Record Book**, UCLA Athletics, owner-supplied PDF.

The year-by-year results section is the row-level backbone through 2024-25. It produced 2,951 recognized competitive UCLA games. Dedicated NCAA/NIT/conference-history/facility material was used as cross-check and enrichment evidence rather than replacing the chronological ledger.

## Completed 2025-26 source

UCLA's official completed 2025-26 schedule supplies the final 36 competitive games (24-12). Two exhibitions are explicitly excluded. The Big Ten Tournament games were played at United Center in Chicago; UCLA's two NCAA Tournament games were played at Xfinity Mobile Arena in Philadelphia.

## Owner-supplied conference-tournament workbook

`Conference_Tournament_Site_Reference(20260902-182553).xlsm` is authorized by the owner **for UCLA's historical conference memberships in this research lane only**. It is not treated as a universal/global source. It supports the Pac-10/Pac-12/Big Ten tournament site sequence where consistent with UCLA game-level evidence.

A material source conflict was preserved rather than silently overwritten: UCLA's own 1989-90 year-by-year footnote says the 1990 Pac-10 Tournament was in **Tempe, Arizona**. Those UCLA rows therefore use the Tempe physical site (ASU/University Activity Center physical identity, research canonicalized as Desert Financial Arena) rather than a conflicting workbook line.

## Early exact-date / H-A-N recovery

The media guide omits exact dates on 498 games through 1944-45 and omits H/A/N on its earliest 110 rows. Research used season-specific historical reconstructions backed by contemporary UCLA/student-newspaper/yearbook/newspaper references, plus authoritative reciprocal institutional schedules where available (including USC, Oregon, Indiana, Purdue, Nebraska, Ohio State and others).

The recovered earliest schedules were matched to UCLA's recognized game universe by opponent and score with occurrence-aware handling for repeated scorelines. Externally documented contests explicitly absent from UCLA's official record book were not added.

Examples of reciprocal recovery include 1937-12-11 at Oregon, 1937-12-28 vs Indiana, 1937-12-29 vs Purdue, 1938-12-26 vs Ohio State in Berkeley, 1938-12-29/30 vs Nebraska, 1940-12-28 vs Indiana, and the December 1941 Purdue/Indiana/Nebraska road sequence.

## Home-facility research

UCLA institutional history establishes Old Men's Gym/Student Activities Center as the principal on-campus pre-Pauley facility, but also documents substantial use of Pan-Pacific Auditorium, Los Angeles Sports Arena, Santa Monica City College, Long Beach-area venues and other sites. A Los Angeles fire-marshal attendance restriction in the 1950s is part of the documented reason UCLA increasingly used larger off-campus facilities.

Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena is the principal 1959-65 home facility, with UCLA guide footnotes identifying individual Santa Monica City College/Men's Gym exceptions. Pauley Pavilion's first regular-season varsity game was 1965-12-03 vs Ohio State. In 2011-12, during Pauley's renovation, UCLA played 14 home games at Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena and four at Honda Center, as documented by UCLA.

The chronology is never used to establish H/A/N. It enriches venue only after HOME is independently known. Prewar rows that cannot safely be tied to one physical building remain `RESEARCHED_UNRESOLVED_HOME_VENUE`.

## NCAA Tournament sites

The UCLA guide's dedicated NCAA history provides dates/opponents/stages and site geography. Physical arenas were resolved against NCAA official tournament site/attendance records and institutional host evidence. Every UCLA NCAA row has physical venue + city + state. The guide prints the 2024-25 NCAA dates with an apparent year typo (`3/20/23`, `3/22/23`); normalized dates are 2025-03-20 and 2025-03-22 from the season context and official 2025 schedule.

The 2021 Indianapolis-area tournament was researched game by game rather than blanket-assigned: Mackey Arena, Hinkle Fieldhouse, Bankers Life Fieldhouse and Lucas Oil Stadium are used as applicable.

## NIT and other postseason

UCLA's 1985 NIT championship run and 1986 first-round NIT game are classified `NIT` with Pauley/MSG sites. Older PCC/AAWU playoff series are `POSTSEASON`, not modern conference-tournament games.

The year-by-year ledger prints `3/13` for the 1985 Montana NIT opener, but UCLA's dedicated NIT box-score heading says **March 14, 1985**, and contemporary reporting independently places the game on Thursday, March 14. The curated date is therefore 1985-03-14 while the raw 3/13 line is preserved.

The 1945-02-17 PCC Southern Division playoff against USC retains UCLA's `OPPONENT_HOME` classification. A secondary site reconstruction conflicts by placing the game at UCLA Men's Gym, so the physical building remains a researched unresolved rather than being invented.

## Administrative-result evidence

The 1976 Oregon State and 1995 California games are on-court UCLA losses with later opponent forfeitures. UCLA's game scores/full box-score or retrospective evidence controls `played_result`; administrative disposition is preserved separately.

## External / reciprocal research source registry

Key authoritative sources used in addition to the owner-supplied UCLA guide and tournament-site workbook include:

- UCLA official completed 2025-26 schedule: `https://uclabruins.com/sports/mens-basketball/schedule/season/2025-26`
- UCLA basketball archives / season-statistics index: `https://uclabruins.com/ucla-basketball-archives`
- NCAA Division I men's basketball statistics/site records: `https://www.ncaa.org/championships/statistics-and-records/mens-basketball/`
- Pac-12 conference history: `https://pac-12.com/sports/2024/6/16/history.aspx`; Big Ten institutional membership history at `https://bigten.org/`.
- Published project reciprocal ledgers for Oregon, Indiana, Purdue, Nebraska, Ohio State, USC and other already-researched programs were used where independently curated assertions matched UCLA by season/opponent/score.
- Event/host institutional records were checked for neutral-event buildings including Lahaina Civic Center, Sullivan Arena, Eugenio Guerra Sports Complex, Cannon Activities Center, Imperial Arena, Orleans Arena and Baoshan Arena.

Secondary historical schedule reconstructions were used only where they preserve citations to contemporary UCLA/student-newspaper/yearbook/newspaper evidence and reconcile exactly to UCLA's recognized opponent/score universe. They did not authorize adding games that UCLA's official record book omits.
